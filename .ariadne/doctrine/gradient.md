# Gradient doctrine

Ariadne work is gap-directed movement under constraints.

- `desired state`: the observable condition the work intends to create;
- `observed state`: what repository evidence currently demonstrates;
- `gap`: a material difference between desired and observed state;
- `movement`: a bounded action expected to reduce a selected gap;
- `constraint`: an invariant or authority boundary the movement must preserve;
- `evaluation`: evidence showing whether the movement reduced the gap and what remains.

Use the loop:

1. Observe the current artifact or behavior.
2. Attribute material gaps to their owning boundary.
3. Compare plausible movements by leverage, risk, cost, and reversibility.
4. Execute one bounded movement.
5. Evaluate its acceptance evidence and side effects.
6. Reassess the objective and remaining gaps.

Store durable gaps with the artifact that owns their resolution. Cross-cutting or consequential proposals belong in a draft plan; accepted executable work belongs in an open plan; deferred directions without authority belong in the roadmap.

Do not treat activity, document volume, test count, or code churn as progress by itself. Progress is a demonstrated reduction in uncertainty, failure risk, or distance from the desired state.
